3.4Methodological Context
Needleman-Wunsch (Global Alignment)

Introduced in 1970, the Needleman-Wunsch algorithm guarantees the construction of the mathematically optimal end-to-end alignment of two sequences. By initializing the boundary cells (representing alignment to initial gaps) with cumulative penalties ($i \times d$ and $j \times d$), it forces alignment across the entire sequence span. Traceback begins at the absolute bottom-rightmost cell $(S1.length, S2.length)$ and returns back to the origin $(0,0)$.

Smith-Waterman (Local Alignment)

Introduced in 1981, the Smith-Waterman algorithm focuses on identifying island regions of high local similarity. To prevent penalizing unrelated terminal sequences, negative cell values are clamped to 0 ($D(i,j) = \max(0, \text{diagonal}, \text{up}, \text{left})$) and boundaries start at 0. Traceback begins at the cell containing the highest score found anywhere in the matrix, and terminates as soon as any cell with a score of 0 is encountered.
