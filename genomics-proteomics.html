5.1The Molecular Dogma

The flow of genetic information is governed by the Central Dogma of Molecular Biology: DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is subsequently translated into functional proteins.

  • DNA (The Genome): The hereditary library containing instructions for life. It is packaged in chromosomes, and the sum of all DNA is the genome.
  • Gene: A specific sequence locus on DNA that encodes instructions for making a protein or functional RNA molecule.
  • mRNA (Transcription): RNA polymerase complements the template DNA strand to synthesize a single-stranded messenger RNA. Thymine (T) is replaced by Uracil (U).
  • Protein (The Proteome): Ribosomes scan mRNA codons (triplets of nucleotides) to pair them with amino acids, constructing polypeptides. The proteome is the collection of all expressed proteins.
5.2Comparative Genomics

Comparative Genomics involves comparing the genomic features of different organisms (genes, regulatory sequences, chromosome structure) to understand evolutionary relationships and identify functional domains.

Within this domain, pairwise sequence alignments (such as the global NW and local SW algorithms implemented in our sequence analysis suite) are vital. By aligning genomes across different species, mutations (mismatches) and insertion/deletions (gaps) are highlighted, allowing researchers to trace phylogenetic ancestry and locate conserved coding islands.
